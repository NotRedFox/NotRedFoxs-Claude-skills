# URL slugs for blog posts

Started 2026-10-01. Last updated 2026-10-01.

## Summary
- Goal: "turn any post title into a short readable URL, in any language, and two posts should never get the same one."
- Works now (checked): English titles, accented letters, runs of punctuation, titles with no Latin letters at all.
- Still broken (checked): German sharp s is dropped. `Straße` gives `strae`.
- Believed but not checked: other letters with no ASCII form (Polish `ł`, Danish `ø`) are dropped the same way.

| Problem | Approaches tried | What ended up working |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Basic slugs | 1 | Lowercase and swap non a-z characters for hyphens |
| 2. Accented letters | 2 | Split accents off (Unicode NFKD) before dropping non-ASCII |
| 3. Non-Latin titles | 2 | Fall back to a short hash of the title |
| 4. German sharp s | 0 | Not fixed, waiting on open question 1 |

## Problems and approaches

### Problem 1: Basic slugs

#### Approach 1.1: Lowercase, then swap runs of anything that isn't a-z or 0-9 for one hyphen. Worked
- What was done: `re.sub(r"[^a-z0-9]+", "-", title.lower()).strip("-")`.
- Why it was chosen: simplest thing that matches the examples in the request.
- Evidence: `slugify("Hello World")` gave `hello-world`. `slugify("Wait... what?!")` gave `wait-what`.
- Why it worked: the `+` turns a run of punctuation into a single hyphen, and `strip` removes hyphens at the ends.
- Kept or undone: Kept.

### Problem 2: Accented letters

#### Approach 2.1: Use approach 1.1 as is. Failed
- What was done: ran approach 1.1 on a French title.
- Why it was chosen: checking whether the basic version already coped.
- Evidence: `slugify("Café au lait")` gave `caf-au-lait`.
- Why it failed: `é` is not in a-z, so it was treated as punctuation. Confirmed by running the regex on `é` alone.
- Kept or undone: Not a code change.

#### Approach 2.2: Split accents off with Unicode NFKD, then drop anything that isn't ASCII. Worked
- What was done: `unicodedata.normalize("NFKD", title).encode("ascii", "ignore")` before the regex.
- Why it was chosen: NFKD turns `é` into `e` plus a separate accent mark, and the mark is the part that gets dropped.
- Evidence: `slugify("Café au lait")` gave `cafe-au-lait`.
- Why it worked: the base letter survives and only the accent is removed.
- Kept or undone: Kept.

### Problem 3: Non-Latin titles

#### Approach 3.1: Fall back to the word `post` when the slug comes out empty. Failed
- What was done: `return slug or "post"`.
- Why it was chosen: after approach 2.2, `slugify("東京")` gave an empty string, and this stopped that.
- Evidence: `slugify("東京")` and `slugify("大阪")` both gave `post`.
- Why it failed: a fixed word is the same for every title, so the second post would overwrite the first.
- Kept or undone: Undone.

#### Approach 3.2: Fall back to `post-` plus the first 8 characters of a SHA-1 hash of the title. Worked
- What was done: `"post-" + hashlib.sha1(title.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()[:8]`.
- Why it was chosen: a hash is different for different titles and the same every time for one title.
- Evidence: `slugify("東京")` gave `post-681ee300`. `slugify("大阪")` gave a different value.
- Why it worked: different input gives different output. Collisions are possible in theory but very unlikely at 8 hex characters for a blog.
- Kept or undone: Kept.

### Problem 4: German sharp s

No approach tried yet. Found while testing problem 2: `slugify("Straße")` gives `strae`, because `ß` has no NFKD split, so it is dropped as non-ASCII. Waiting on open question 1.

## What worked
- 1.1 Regex with `+`: collapses punctuation runs into one hyphen.
- 2.2 NFKD before dropping non-ASCII: keeps the base letter of accented characters.
- 3.2 Hash fallback: unique slug for titles with no Latin letters.

## What didn't, do not retry
- 2.1 Plain a-z regex on its own: drops every accented letter.
- 3.1 Fixed fallback word: every non-Latin title collides.
