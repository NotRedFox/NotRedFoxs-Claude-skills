# Research notes

Which finding shaped which part of the skill. Links are at the end of `SKILL.md`.

| Finding | Evidence | What the skill does with it |
|---|---|---|
| Studying worked examples beats solving from scratch, for beginners | g = 0.48 over 55 maths studies (Barbieri et al. 2023) | Walkthroughs come before practice |
| Remove worked steps gradually; guidance that helps beginners gets in the way later | Atkinson, Renkl and Merrill 2003; Kalyuga et al. 2003 | Show a pattern in full once, then leave steps to the learner; Simple, Normal and Deep switch |
| Labelling steps by purpose helps transfer | Margulieux et al. 2012; Morrison et al. 2015 | Every walkthrough step has a purpose label |
| Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make beat normal lessons | 493 students, 13 schools (Sentance et al. 2019) | Guess-first cards on maps and replays; asking before running tests |
| Being tested beats rereading after a delay | Roediger and Karpicke 2006; Adesope et al. 2017 | Quick checks, and a warm-up question on your next visit |
| Being prompted to explain helps | g = 0.55 over 64 reports (Bisra et al. 2018) | Explain it yourself before seeing the model answer |
| Parsons puzzles take up to half the time of writing code, with similar learning | Ericson, Margulieux and Rick 2017 | Put-the-lines-in-order exercises with one line that doesn't belong |
| Beginners struggle to trace code; teach tracing first | Lister et al. 2004; Xie et al. 2019 | Request traces before hands-on tasks |
| How people use a visualization matters more than how it looks | Hundhausen et al. 2002; Naps et al. 2002 | Every area and replay step asks you to do something |
| Newcomers settle in through small real tasks and feedback | Dagenais et al. 2010; Steinmacher et al. 2014 | One safe task per area; a clear starting point |
| Concept maps help memory and transfer | 55 studies (Nesbit and Adesope 2006) | The map with labelled connections |
| Showing a system at a few fixed zoom levels helps people find their way | C4 model | Layered maps you can drill into |
| Open-ended AI help raised practice scores 48% and cut exam scores 17%; a hint-only tutor didn't | Bastani et al. 2025 | Hints before answers |
| Junior developers who asked AI for explanations kept their skills; those who handed work over scored under 40% | Shen and Tamkin 2026 | Code never arrives without an explanation; replays of agent work |
| AI gives struggling beginners an illusion of competence | Prather et al. 2024 | Ask for the learner's plan first; check understanding at the end |
| Trust in AI lowers critical thinking | Lee et al. 2025 | Learner verifies one of the agent's claims |
| Trying before being taught helps understanding | Sinha and Kapur 2021 | "How would you approach this?" before building |
